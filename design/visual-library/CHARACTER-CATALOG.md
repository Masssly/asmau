# Reusable Character Catalogue

This catalogue defines character families, not final named characters. Names, biographies and exact historical identities should be created per game.

## Shared design requirements

- Black African characters throughout the initial Asmau visual universe.
- Natural diversity in complexion, facial structure, age, height and build.
- Respectful, non-caricatured representation.
- Clothing grounded in a named region and period.
- Consistent proportions and rendering across the library.
- Each production character should have front, side and back views, expression sheets, colour/material callouts and scale references.

## Character families

### 1. Children
**Purpose:** player companions, learners, family members and local children.  
**Design:** expressive but not babyish; varied hair textures and styles; practical clothing; clear silhouettes.  
**Variants:** younger child, older child, sibling pair, child learner, child traveller.

### 2. Parents and family elders
**Purpose:** family scenes, guidance, care and community storytelling.  
**Design:** warm and grounded; clothing and head coverings should vary by region, role and period.  
**Variants:** father, mother, grandparent, aunt/uncle, guardian.

### 3. Scholars and teachers
**Purpose:** Islamic learning, manuscript culture, advice and community leadership.  
**Design:** dignified, approachable and intellectually engaged; books and writing tools should be culturally researched.  
**Wardrobe references:** regionally appropriate robes, turbans or head coverings, modest layered garments.

### 4. Community leaders and officials
**Purpose:** civic life, negotiation, administration and public gatherings.  
**Design:** confident but not automatically regal; status can be expressed through fabric quality, tailoring and restrained accessories.

### 5. Merchants and craftspeople
**Purpose:** markets, trade, workshops and everyday economy.  
**Design:** varied body types and ages; practical garments; baskets, scales, pottery, leather goods and textiles.

### 6. Farmers, herders and workers
**Purpose:** agriculture, food production, water, animal care and ordinary village life.  
**Design:** practical, weather-appropriate clothing and tools; portray skill and agency rather than as background scenery.

### 7. Travellers and caravan members
**Purpose:** journeys, trade routes, desert crossings and encounters.  
**Design:** layered garments, head wraps, water containers, bags and practical footwear. Avoid costume clichés; research the route and period.

### 8. Guards and attendants
**Purpose:** palace, city gate and caravan scenes.  
**Design:** role-appropriate, non-threatening at child scale; equipment must be historically researched and not gratuitously militarised.

## Wardrobe reference notes

Babban riga, embroidered robes, turbans, wrappers and veils are useful references, but each item needs a region/period tag. Do not assign one style to every Black Muslim character.

## Prophet depiction policy for Yusuf

Do not create a visible character model, portrait, face, silhouette or body representation for Prophet Yusuf. Use:
- First-person or over-the-shoulder environmental framing that does not reveal him.
- A point of light or dream imagery only when it does not imply a literal depiction.
- His voice only if the project has an approved narration approach; otherwise use narrator-led storytelling.
- Objects, spatial composition and the reactions of other characters to communicate his presence.

The same question should be reviewed separately for any other prophets in future games.
