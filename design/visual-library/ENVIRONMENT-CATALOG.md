# Environment and Prop Catalogue

## Design principle

Build environments as places people actually live and work. A settlement needs homes, paths, water, storage, workshops, gathering spaces and ordinary details, not only grand monuments.

## Environment families

### Sahelian settlement
- Earthen compound walls and shaded courtyards.
- Homes with regionally researched forms.
- Trees, planted areas and practical water features.
- Paths, gates, storage and communal spaces.

### Hausa city quarter
- Dense street layout and compounds.
- Decorated gates and doors.
- Market stalls and craft workshops.
- Mosque and learning spaces, with researched local forms.
- Public gathering and water points.

### Scholarly and learning spaces
- Study circles, manuscript shelves, writing boards and book storage.
- Materials and writing tools must be researched for the represented period.
- Qur'anic writing must be verified and treated respectfully.

### Market
- Textile, pottery, leather, food and household-goods stalls.
- Shade canopies and woven baskets.
- Clear routes and interactive objects for gameplay.
- Diverse merchants and customers.

### Desert and caravan route
- Dunes, rocky escarpments, dry valleys and oasis vegetation.
- Rest stops, wells, tents and caravan equipment.
- Weather and vegetation should fit the actual geographic inspiration.

### River and granary environment
- Riverbanks, boats, irrigation, fields and storage buildings.
- For Yusuf's story, research ancient Nile Valley agriculture and storage separately from later Hausa/Sokoto forms.

## Reusable prop families

- Pottery: water jars, bowls, storage vessels.
- Textiles: folded cloth, bolts, rugs, woven mats.
- Trade: baskets, scales, weights, sacks and merchant tables.
- Domestic: stools, low tables, lamps, cooking vessels and storage chests.
- Learning: manuscripts, writing boards, reed pens and ink containers.
- Travel: water skins, bags, sandals, packs and caravan equipment.
- Agriculture: grain, baskets, tools, storage bins and irrigation details.

## Yusuf-specific historical caution

Yusuf's Quranic story is associated with Egypt. A deliberate African reinterpretation is welcome, but do not present Sokoto-era architecture, clothing or objects as historically accurate ancient Egyptian artefacts. Maintain a separate research sheet for ancient Egypt/Nile Valley material culture and tag any West African-inspired elements as artistic interpretation.
