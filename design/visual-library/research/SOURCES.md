from the library's own documents plus general knowledge, and is marked by confidence. No citation has been
invented. Before any asset is marked approved, every line in the "to verify" list needs a real source.

## Claims used in the wardrobe brief and their confidence

| Claim | Confidence | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Hausa men wear a large, wide-sleeved robe commonly called babban riga | high | general knowledge, widely documented |
| Such robes are often decorated with embroidery at the chest, neck and pocket | high | general knowledge |
| Narrow-strip handwoven cotton, sewn into cloth, is a long West African tradition | high | general knowledge |
| Indigo dyeing is strongly associated with Kano | medium | general knowledge, needs citation |
| Specific embroidery motif names (e.g. the two long blades, "aska biyu") | low | recalled, not verified |
| Roundel at back of neck as a standard feature | low | recalled, not verified |
| White brimmed embroidered cap as men's headwear | medium | general knowledge, needs citation |
| Girl's outfit (blouse, wrapper, head scarf) | low | **design proposal**, not documented practice |

## To verify with real sources (open task)

1. Babban riga construction, embroidery layout and pocket position, from a museum collection or published
   dress-history source whose image rights allow *written observation* (not copying the image).
2. Women's and girls' dress in the Sokoto/Kano region in the relevant period.
3. Cap forms and what they signal (age, status).
4. Whether the indigo-and-undyed stripe system is regional, and its correct name.
5. Dagbamba (Dagomba) dress and architecture, see `DAGBAMBA-PROFILE.md`.

## Rules for adding a source

Record title, author, year, URL or shelfmark, access date, what was learned, and the image licence. Do not copy
protected images into the repository; link and describe.
