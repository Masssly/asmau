Count with Me

Tap, count and say Mashallah!

Ages 1½–5
Mashallah!

For parents

  • What it teaches. Touching one thing for each number, the number words from one to twenty, what the numerals look like, and that the last number said is how many there are. From “Count to 10” up, the game also asks “How many?” with two or three answers to choose from; a wrong answer is never marked, the game just counts along with your child.
  • Four levels. Count to 5 and to 10 are for the littlest: everything is big. To 15 and to 20 put more than ten things on the screen, so each is smaller; they suit a child of about three and up.
  • Nothing can go wrong. There is no score, no clock and nothing to lose. Every round ends with “Mashallah!” and the next one starts by itself.
  • Arabic. The Arabic numeral and number word are shown under the big number. They are never read out by a computer voice, because a guessed pronunciation is worse than silence. They are spoken only if you record them yourself in Record your voice; the English words there can be replaced with your own voice too (today they are a computer placeholder).
  • Sound. The switches above turn the voice, the sound effects and the music off one by one. Families differ on music, so it has its own switch and the game works fully without it.
  • Pictures. No people are drawn. The pictures are moons, stars, lanterns, prayer beads, raindrops, flowers, dates, apples and one simple cartoon sheep.
  • Leaving. Press and hold “Asmau” in the top corner to go back to all the games. It needs a long press so that little fingers do not leave by accident.
