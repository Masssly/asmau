"They pay great attention to memorizing the Holy Qur'an. If their children appear to be backward in learning it they put shackles on them and do not remove them till they learn it." He saw the qadi's children shackled on the day of Eid, and a well-dressed young man with a heavy shackle on his foot (vol. IV, p. 965). The game says only that the children worked hard.

He also listed "bad practices" in Mali: servant women and slave-girls who went unclothed, people putting dust on their heads before the Sultan, and eating carrion, dogs and donkeys (vol. IV, pp. 965-966). These are a Moroccan judge's views of a culture not his own.
