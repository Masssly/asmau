Musa

The Story of Musa

Twenty-five chapters. About a hundred things to do. Play a little every day.

Ages 3–10

Under 5: play it together with a grown-up

For parents
  • The story of Musa is spread over many surahs. This game follows Surah Al-Qasas (28:3–43) and Surah Ta-Ha (20:9–98), with passages from surahs 2, 5, 7, 10, 18, 26, 40 and 66. It is a large part of what the Qur’an tells of Musa, not all of it.
  • Told more gently than the verses tell them: Pharaoh’s order against the baby boys (28:4), the man Musa struck (28:15), Pharaoh’s threats to the magicians (20:71), the drowning (26:66) and Musa’s anger at his brother (7:150). Not told to children at all: the boy in 18:74, which the game calls “a story for when you are older”.
  • From the commentary or hadith and not the verses, among others: the queen’s name, the hot coal, the heavy rock at the well, the pebbles on the path, the twelve paths through the sea, the frogs in the pots, the name al-Khidr and the bird on the boat. Where the early scholars differed, the chapter note says so.
  • Shown in this story: Prophet Musa and Prophet Harun (peace be upon them), al-Khidr, and the people around them. Allah’s speech to Musa is shown only as a fire and a light. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is mentioned once, at the fast of Ashura.
  • It is long on purpose: about a hundred activities over 25 chapters. Nobody is meant to finish it in one sitting. A child of 3 or 4 will need a grown-up for the ordering, sorting and matching games.
