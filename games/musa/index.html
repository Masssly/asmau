Musa

The Story of Musa

Twenty-five chapters. About a hundred things to do. Play a little every day.

Ages 3–10

Under 5: play it together with a grown-up

For parents
  • This is a retelling in simple words, not a translation. The story of Musa is spread over many surahs; this game follows Surah Al-Qasas (28:3–43) and Surah Ta-Ha (20:9–98), with passages from surahs 2, 5, 7, 10, 18, 26, 40 and 66. Every chapter was checked against M. A. S. Abdel Haleem’s English translation. Under each scene, “For parents: this chapter” says what the verses say beyond the child’s version and links to them.
  • Details that are not in the verses are marked on their scene with a small label. They come from Ibn Kathir’s tafsir (abridged English), or from hadith quoted in it, and each chapter note names who the report is from: for example the queen’s name, the heavy rock at the well, the pebbles on the path, the twelve paths through the sea, the frogs in the pots, the name al-Khidr and the bird on the boat. Where the early scholars differed, the note says so.
  • Some parts are told more gently than the verses tell them: Pharaoh’s order against the baby boys (28:4), the man Musa struck (28:15), Pharaoh’s threats to the magicians (20:71), the drowning (26:66), and Musa’s anger at his brother (7:150). One event is not told to children at all: the boy in 18:74, which the game calls “a story for when you are older”. Each chapter note says exactly what was softened or left out.
  • People are shown, all with dark skin, including Prophet Musa and Prophet Harun (peace be upon them). Muslims differ on whether prophets should be shown, and these games show them. Allah is never shown: His speech to Musa appears only as a fire and a light. No angel is shown. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is mentioned once (the fast of Ashura) and is not shown.
  • The story’s pictures are not made yet, so for now each scene is drawn with simple icons. When the pictures arrive they will be AI-generated, like the Yusuf ones, in the same West African style.
  • It is long on purpose: about a hundred activities. Nobody is meant to finish it in one sitting. The game remembers the place, and “☰” opens any chapter. A child of 3 or 4 will need a grown-up for the ordering, sorting and matching games. Every activity can be skipped.
  • The music, the sounds of each place (the river, the wind, the fire, a far-off crowd) and the effects are made by the browser as you play; there are no recordings of instruments or voices in them. 🎵 turns the music off on its own, and 🔊 turns off the voice, the effects and the sounds of the place. Both choices are remembered on this device.
  • English is read by a computer voice, standing in until a real one is recorded. Arabic is never read by a computer voice. Record your own voice and the game uses it straight away on this device.
