For parents
  • Nana Asma’u bint Usman dan Fodio (about 1793 to 1864) was a scholar, poet and teacher in Sokoto, in what is now northern Nigeria. She trained women teachers who took learning to the villages, and wrote more than sixty works in Arabic, Fulfulde and Hausa. This site is named after her.
  • This is history, not the Qur’an. The facts come from Jean Boyd and Beverly Mack, The Collected Works of Nana Asma’u (1997), which draws on the Sokoto historian Waziri Junaidu, and from other sources each chapter names. Where the sources differ on a date, the chapter note says so, and the game gives no year.
  • Not told to children: the war years in detail (the Sokoto jihad, 1804 to 1808), and the captives who were part of that history. Each chapter note says what was left out.
  • Shown in this story: Nana Asma’u, her father the Shehu Usman dan Fodio, her family and her women teachers. In chapter 2, Asma bint Abi Bakr, a Companion of the Prophet ﷺ, is shown only from behind and by her hands.
  • For ages 4 to 10. Under 5, sit with your child: the journey and the hard years may need explaining.
