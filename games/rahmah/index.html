For parents
  • Rahmah El Yunusiyyah (1900 to 1969), of the Minangkabau people of West Sumatra, Indonesia, opened Diniyah Putri in Padang Panjang on 1 November 1923: generally thought to be the first Islamic school for girls in the Indies. It is still open. Al-Azhar gave her the title Syaikhah, and in 2025 Indonesia named her a National Hero.
  • This is modern history, not the Qur’an. The facts come from published histories of her life; the notes for each page name them. Where the sources disagree (her birthday; the year of the al-Azhar title) the game gives no date and the notes say so.
  • Not told to children, but in the page notes for you: her marriage when she was very young and her divorce, the Japanese occupation and the war, her months in a Dutch prison, and her years in politics.
  • Shown in this story: Rahmah and the Minangkabau people as themselves, every woman’s hair covered, as in her own photographs.
