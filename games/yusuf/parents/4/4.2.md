The picture shows Yusuf sitting among the bundles of goods on a camel. It follows the verse 'They hid him like a piece of merchandise' (12:19). The scholars explain that hiding in different ways, and none of them says he was buried under the goods. So the picture shows only what their explanations share: he was carried along like something to be sold, not like a traveller.

Al-Tafsir al-Muyassar: the man who drew the water and his companions hid Yusuf from the rest of the travellers and did not show him to them, saying that he was goods they had been given to trade.

Al-Tabari, in his tafsir Jami' al-Bayan, records four explanations from the early scholars. First, from Mujahid and as-Suddi: the man with the bucket and his companions told the rest of the caravan that the boy was goods the people of the water had given them to sell, because they feared the others would ask for a share if they knew the men had found and bought him. Second, also from Mujahid: the traders hid him from one another. Third, from Qatadah: they kept his sale secret. Fourth, from Ibn Abbas: it was Yusuf's brothers who hid that he was their brother and said he was their slave; Yusuf kept quiet about it, fearing they would kill him, and chose to be sold.

Al-Tabari prefers the first explanation, because these words come straight after the man who found him and so most likely speak about him and his companions. The commentary (Ibn Kathir) quotes only the explanation from Ibn Abbas.

Al-Tabari also reports from Mujahid that the brothers followed the caravan, telling the men to keep a close watch on the boy so that he would not run away, until he was stood up for sale in Egypt. That is the market on page 4.4: the first time he is shown openly.
