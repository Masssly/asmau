How could Yusuf ask for his youngest brother without giving himself away? The Qur'an tells only that he recognised them, gave them their grain and then asked them to bring 'the brother you left with your father' (12:58–59). This page comes from the commentaries, so the game says 'It is told that'.

Al-Tafsir al-Muyassar: the brothers had told him that they had a brother from their father whom they had not brought with them.

As-Suddi, reported by al-Tabari and in the commentary (Ibn Kathir): Yusuf questioned them and said they might be spies. They answered that they were brothers, sons of a truthful man, Yaqub of the land of Canaan; that they had been twelve; that one of them, the one their father loved most, had died in the desert; and that their father kept that boy's full brother at home with him. In al-Tabari's report, Yusuf then asks how their father can be truthful if he loves the youngest more than the older ones, and tells them to bring him so that he can see him.

Notice what the brothers say about the lost brother: they still tell their own story of what happened to Yusuf, to Yusuf himself, without knowing who he is. Ask your child: how do you think Yusuf felt, hearing that?
