For parents

Free Islamic stories and games for children. Tap a topic.

Your voice is the best voice for your child

The voice reading the stories today is a computer. It is there so that every game works from the first day, and it is not meant to stay. Children listen differently when it is a parent or a grandparent telling the story. Record the stories yourself, and the game plays your voice from then on.

In your own language

Read each line in whatever language your family speaks: Dagbanli, Hausa, Twi, Yoruba, Swahili, Arabic, German, English or any other. The words on the screen stay in English; what your child hears can be yours. Arabic is only ever heard from a person’s recording, never from a computer.

How it works

  1. Open Record your voice and choose a story.
  2. Each line of the story is shown. Press Record, read it, press Stop. Listen back, and redo it if you like.
  3. Open the story: your lines play straight away. Lines you have not recorded yet still use the computer voice, so you can record a few each evening.

On your other devices, and for grandparents

Recordings stay on the device you made them on. To hear them on a phone and a tablet too, make a family code in the recorder. Anyone with the code, a grandparent far away for example, can record on their own phone and the whole family hears it. Recordings are locked on the device before they travel; if you lose the code, the copy kept online is gone.

Private by design

Most apps for children collect data, show adverts or ask for an account. Asmau does none of that, on purpose.

  • No account, no sign-in, no email. Open it and play.
  • No adverts and nothing to buy inside the games.
  • No tracking. No analytics, no advertising cookies, nothing loaded from advertising or social media companies.
  • What your child does stays on the device: where they got to, the settings, and your recordings.
  • Recordings leave the device only if you choose, with a family code, and they are locked on the device before they are sent, so nobody else, including whoever runs Asmau, can listen to them.

Like any website, the places that serve Asmau’s pages, pictures and sounds (GitHub and Cloudflare) see that a device asked for a file. They are not told who your child is or what they do in the games.

Where the stories come from

Each story from the Qur’an is a retelling in simple words, not a translation. Every chapter is checked against M. A. S. Abdel Haleem’s English translation. Any detail that is not in the verses comes from a named commentary, mostly Ibn Kathir’s tafsir (abridged English), or from a hadith quoted in it, and it carries a small label on its scene.

Under every scene, “For parents: this chapter” tells you what the verses say beyond the child’s version, what was told more gently or left out, whose report a detail comes from, and links to the verses, so you can answer your child’s questions.

Who is shown, and how

People are shown with their faces, all with dark skin, prophets included (peace be upon them). Muslims differ on whether prophets should be shown; these games show them. Allah is never shown, no angel is shown, and Prophet Muhammad ﷺ never appears. Each story’s first page names who appears in it.

Playing together

  • Ages are on every game. Count with Me is for the littlest, from about 1½. The stories are for about 3 to 10; under 5, sit with your child.
  • A little at a time. The stories are long on purpose: a chapter or two a day is plenty, and the game remembers where your child got to.
  • Talk about it. Ask what someone felt, or what your child would have done. The note under each scene gives you the details.
  • How to play. Everything reacts to a touch. Swipe a scene, or use the big buttons, to turn the page. ☰ opens the list of chapters and closes it again.
  • Nothing to lose. Nothing is timed and nothing is marked wrong for long. If a child is stuck, “Skip” appears after a quiet moment.

Sound and volume

Every story has three switches at the top: 🎵 music, 🍃 the sounds of the place and the effects, and 🗣️ the voice. Families differ on music in children’s media, so it has its own switch, and every game works fully without it. The music and sounds are made by the browser as you play; there are no recordings of instruments.

Without internet

After the first visit, everything works with no connection. On a phone or tablet, add Asmau to the home screen from the browser’s menu (“Add to Home Screen”, or “Install app”) and it opens like an app.

If a device ever looks out of date, refresh it here. Recordings and progress are not touched.

The pictures

The story pictures are made with an AI image generator (Google Gemini), from approved drawings of each person so that they look the same from scene to scene. They are an artist’s-eye view in a West African style: nobody knows what these people or places looked like, and the clothes and buildings are not a reconstruction of the past. When a picture is replaced, the old one is kept.
