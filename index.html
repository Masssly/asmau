Your voice is the best voice for your child

Read a story aloud once, in any language, and the game plays your voice from then on.

Until then, who reads?

How it works
  1. Choose a story. Its pages come one at a time, each with its picture and its words.
  2. Tap the red button, read the words, tap it again. You hear yourself at once; tap Again to redo it, or go on to the next page. Every page is saved as you go.
  3. Open the story: your pages play straight away. Pages you have not recorded yet keep the computer voice, so you can record a few each evening; Asmau remembers where you stopped.
Your language, and the Arabic words

Read each line in whatever language your family speaks: Dagbanli, Hausa, Twi, Yoruba, Swahili, Arabic, German, English or any other. The words on the screen stay in English; what your child hears can be yours. The computer voice also says the Arabic words and prayers, and may not say every one perfectly; your recording replaces it there too.

Why not the computer voice?

The voice reading the stories today is a computer. It is there so that every game works from the first day, and it is not meant to stay. Children listen differently when it is a parent or a grandparent telling the story. Your own recordings always play first.

Phones, tablets and grandparents

Recordings stay on the device you made them on. To hear them on a phone and a tablet too, turn on your other devices in My recordings and scan the code with the other phone. A grandparent far away can do the same, record on their own phone, and the whole family hears it. Recordings are locked on the device before they travel; keep the family code safe, because without it the copy kept online is gone.

Private by design

Most apps for children collect data, show adverts or ask for an account. Asmau does none of that, on purpose.

  • No account, no sign-in, no email. Open it and play.
  • No adverts and nothing to buy inside the games.
  • No tracking. We only count downloads per game and country, never by whom.
  • What your child does stays on the device.
  • Recordings leave the device only if you choose, locked so nobody else can listen.
The details

No analytics run on the device, no cookies, and nothing is loaded from advertising or social media companies. We count only how often each game’s pictures and voices are downloaded, and from which country, never by whom.

What stays on the device: where your child got to, the settings, and your recordings. Recordings travel only if you turn on your other devices, with a family code; they are locked on the device before they are sent, so nobody else, including whoever runs Asmau, can listen to them.

Like any website, the places that serve Asmau’s pages, pictures and sounds (GitHub and Cloudflare) see that a device asked for a file. They are not told who your child is or what they do in the games.

Where donations go

Donations from families pay for the storage, the pictures, and the time it takes to make each new story carefully. Any amount helps. Donating does not unlock any game; the only thing it changes is the space for your recordings, if you keep them online and run out. You never have to pay or give anything to use Asmau.

Why mistakes matter to us

Every story is checked, but a second pair of eyes matters, most of all from someone with knowledge of the Qur’an and tafsir. Every story page also has a link to tell us about that page.

Where the stories come from

Each story is retold in simple words from the Qur’an, with a note for parents under every page.

Parents’ guides: every note of a game on one page.

    How each story is checked

    Each story from the Qur’an is a retelling in simple words, not a translation. Every chapter is checked against M. A. S. Abdel Haleem’s English translation. Any detail that is not in the verses comes from a named commentary, mostly Ibn Kathir’s tafsir (abridged English), or from a hadith quoted in it, and it carries a small label on its scene. The prophets’ stories also use Shia sources, and where the two differ the note says so.

    What the note for parents tells you

    Under every page, “📖 Note for parents” tells you what the verses say beyond the child’s version, what was told more gently or left out, whose report a detail comes from, and links to the verses, so you can answer your child’s questions.

    Who is shown, and how

    Faces are shown, prophets included (peace be upon them), so children can follow the story. Muslims differ on this.

    • The prophets’ stories show everyone with dark skin, as Black African families.
    • The great Muslims are drawn as the people they really were: Umm Ayman from Abyssinia, Ibn Battuta from Morocco, each land’s people as themselves.
    • Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is never shown, at any age. Where he is present, the picture leaves him out and a soft breeze passes over it. His family and Companions are shown.
    • Allah is never shown. Angels appear only as light with wings, and Iblis only as smoke and flame, never with a face.
    • Every woman’s and girl’s hair is covered.

    Each story’s first page names who appears in it.

    Playing together

    The stories are for about 3 to 10; under 5, sit with your child. A chapter or two a day is plenty.

    Ages

    Ages are on every game. Count with Me is for the littlest, from about 1½. The stories are for about 3 to 10; under 5, sit with your child.

    A little at a time

    The stories are long on purpose: a chapter or two a day is plenty, and the game remembers where your child got to.

    Talk about it

    Ask what someone felt, or what your child would have done. The note under each scene gives you the details.

    How to play

    Everything reacts to a touch. Swipe a scene, or use the big buttons, to turn the page. ☰ opens the list of chapters and closes it again.

    Nothing to lose

    Nothing is timed and nothing is marked wrong for long. If a child is stuck, “Skip” appears after a quiet moment.

    Sound and volume

    Every story has three buttons at the top: 🎵 music, 🍃 sounds, 🗣️ voice. Each tap is a step louder; after the loudest it turns off. The same buttons, here:

    Who reads the stories

    Your own recordings always play first.

    Why music has its own button

    🍃 covers the sounds of the place and the effects. Families differ on music in children’s media, so music has its own button, and every game works fully without it. After the loudest step a button turns off, and the next tap starts again from the quietest. The music and sounds are made by the browser as you play; there are no recordings of instruments.

    Without internet

    Asmau keeps each page once it has been played, so it works again without a connection and uses little data. Going somewhere without signal? Save the games first, on Wi-Fi.

      How much data it uses

      Data costs money, so Asmau downloads only what your child plays: each page is kept once it has been played, and works again without a connection. The first visit fetches only the front page, about 1.5 MB. A game your child has played keeps the pages played so far; download it to have all of it (here or in the game’s own “For parents” box), and delete it when you are done to free the space. Progress and your recordings are always kept.

      Put Asmau on your home screen

      On a phone or tablet, add Asmau to the home screen from the browser’s menu (“Add to Home Screen”, or “Install app”) and it opens like an app. On an iPhone or iPad this also keeps your family code, recordings and your child’s place safe: Safari on its own clears a website’s saved things after a week without a visit.

      Looks out of date?

      If a device ever looks out of date, refresh it here. Downloaded games, recordings, your family code and progress are all kept.

      The pictures

      The story pictures are made with an AI image generator (Google Gemini), from approved drawings of each person so that they look the same from scene to scene. They are an artist’s-eye view in a West African style: nobody knows what these people or places looked like, and the clothes and buildings are not a reconstruction of the past. When a picture is replaced, the old one is kept.
