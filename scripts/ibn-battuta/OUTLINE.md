# Ibn Battuta: outline (for the owner's approval)

Eleven chapters, ages 5 to 9, in "Great Muslims" (born 1304, so after Umm Ayman and before Nana Asma'u). A journey game:
a map that fills in chapter by chapter, from Tangier to China and back to Mali. Every chapter names its page in the
Rihla. Sources: scripts/ibn-battuta/RESEARCH.md. People drawn as each land's people.

1. **Leaving home** (I, 8-9): a young man of 21 in Tangier leaves alone for the Hajj, "as birds forsake their nests";
   his parents are sad to see him go. *Activity: pack his bag (sort: what does a traveller need?).*
2. **The old man of Alexandria** (I, 23-24): "Visit my brothers in India, in Sind and in China, and greet them from
   me." It is told he did, years later. *Activity: find India and China on the map.*
3. **The broken dish** (I, 238-239): in Damascus a boy breaks a dish; the town has a fund that buys him a new one,
   "a mender of hearts". *Activity: put the pieces back together (puzzle). Activity: kind or not?*
4. **Makkah** (I): his first Hajj; people from every land in white. *Activity: the steps of the Hajj in order.*
5. **Across the deserts and seas** (II): caravans and ships; camels, the sea, a storm. *Activity: lead the caravan
   (board). Activity: count the camels.*
6. **India** (III): Delhi and its Sultan; Ibn Battuta becomes a judge. *Activity: a fair or unfair? choice.*
7. **The islands** (IV): the Maldives, coconut palms and the sea. *Activity: catch the coconuts.*
8. **Sumatra** (IV, 875-877): boats with coconuts, bananas and mangoes; a kind Sultan who loves learning and walks to
   Friday prayer on foot. *Activity: match the fruits. Activity: a choice question.*
9. **China** (IV, 889-893): paper money the size of a hand, porcelain, "the safest and best country for the
   traveller". *Activity: tap to pay with paper money (count).*
10. **Home again** (IV, 920): back in Morocco after 24 years; his mother has died, may Allah have mercy on her. Told
    gently. *Activity: the way home in order.*
11. **Mali, and the book** (IV, 965-966; I): across the Sahara to Mali: no one fears robbers; everyone in clean white
    at Friday prayer; children learning the Qur'an by heart. Then in Fez the Sultan has his travels written down: the
    book we still read. *Activity: tell the whole journey in order, on the map.*

For the parents' notes: dates; who Ibn Juzayy was; the saints he visited (the book's own words: "it is told"); the
shackled children of Mali and what he said; that some historians doubt parts of the journey (with the source); the
bad things he also wrote (left out of the game).
Left out: his marriages, slaves and the wars he saw; the "what I found bad" in Mali.
