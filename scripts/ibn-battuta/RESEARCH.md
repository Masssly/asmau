# Ibn Battuta: sources

Main source: his own book, the *Rihla* (dictated in Fez in 1355-56 to Ibn Juzayy, who wrote it up), in the
standard English translation: H. A. R. Gibb (vols. I-III, Hakluyt Society 1958-71) and C. F. Beckingham
(vol. IV, 1994), *The Travels of Ibn Battuta, A.D. 1325-1354* (archive.org "travels-of-ibn-battuta"). Page numbers
below are the Hakluyt page numbers printed in that edition. Read in the text itself on 16 November 2026.

## Episodes for children (all in the Rihla)

- **Leaving Tangier** (I, pp. 8-9): left on 2 Rajab 725 (June 1325) for the Hajj and to visit the Prophet's tomb
  at Madina. "I set out alone, having neither fellow-traveller ... nor caravan ... I braced my resolution to quit all
  my dear ones ... and forsook my home as birds forsake their nests. My parents being yet in the bonds of life, it
  weighed sorely upon me to part from them." He was twenty-two (lunar years). Ibn Juzayy's note: born at Tangier,
  17 Rajab 703 (25 February 1304).
- **The old man of Alexandria** (I, pp. 23-24): the imam Burhan al-Din the Lame said: "I see that you are fond of
  travelling"; "You must certainly, if God will, visit my brother Farid al-Din in India, and my brother Rukn al-Din
  Zakariya in Sind, and my brother Burhan al-Din in China, and ... convey to them a greeting from me." Ibn Battuta:
  "my wanderings never ceased until I had met these three that he named and conveyed his greeting to them." (The
  book calls this a karama, a wonder granted to a saint: "it is told".)
- **The broken dish of Damascus** (I, pp. 238-239): a slave-boy dropped a Chinese porcelain platter and it broke; the
  crowd told him to take the pieces to the custodian of the endowment for utensils, who gave him enough to buy a new
  one. "This endowment is one of the best of good works ... The benefaction is thus indeed a mender of hearts."
  Damascus also had endowments for travellers (food, clothing, the journey home) and for paving the streets.
- **Makkah and the Hajj** (vol. I): his first pilgrimage, 1326. (Pages to note when the chapter is written.)
- **The Sultan of Sumatra** (IV, pp. 875-877): the city of Sumutra (Samudra, in Aceh) with wooden walls and towers;
  people came out in little boats with coconuts, bananas, mangoes and fish. Sultan al-Malik al-Zahir, "one of the
  noblest and most generous of kings, a Shafi'i ... a lover of jurists, who come to his audiences for the recitation
  of the Qur'an and for discussions ... He is unassuming and walks to the Friday prayer on foot." Greeting is by
  shaking hands. (Leave out: his raids on unbelievers.)
- **China** (IV, pp. 889-893): paper money "pieces of paper the size of the palm of the hand"; "China is the safest
  and best country for the traveller." Porcelain.
- **Home** (IV, p. 920): at Taza "I learnt that my mother had died of the plague, God Most High have mercy upon her";
  he reached Fez in Sha'ban 750 (late 1349).
- **Mali** (IV, pp. 965-966, "what I found good"): "their avoidance of injustice; there is no people more averse to
  it"; "the universal security in their country, for neither the traveller nor the resident there has to fear thieves
  or bandits"; the dense crowds at Friday prayer, "clean white clothes on Fridays; if one of them has only a
  threadbare shirt he washes it"; "They pay great attention to memorizing the Holy Qur'an." Mansa Sulayman was the
  sultan (Mansa Musa's brother). (Leave out: the shackled children, which a parents' note can mention, and the
  section "what I found bad".)
- **The book** (I, Ibn Juzayy's introduction): the Sultan of Fez had Ibn Juzayy write down what he told.

  the Volga). Name a source before saying so (Ross Dunn, *The Adventures of Ibn Battuta*, 1986, is on archive.org).
