# Rahmah El Yunusiyyah: outline (for the owner's approval)

Eleven chapters, ages 5 to 9, in "Great Muslims" (born 1900: after Nana Asma'u). Set in West Sumatra, Indonesia.
Theme: a girl's right to learn, and not giving up. Sources: scripts/rahmah/RESEARCH.md; each chapter names its source.

1. **A girl in the mountains** (1900): Padang Panjang among the mountains of West Sumatra; a family of scholars; her
   father a judge. *Activity: find the pairs (things of her home: mountains, rice fields, a curved-roof house).*
2. **Learning to read**: her father died when she was six; his students taught her; she learned to read and write in
   two scripts, and learned to help mothers and babies. *Activity: sort: things we learn at school / at home.*
3. **No more school**: she had to stop school when she was very young, but she kept learning in study circles.
   *Activity: a choice question.* (Her marriage: parents' note.)
4. **Back to school**: at her brother's school she gathered the girls to study together, and saw that girls needed
   their own school. *Activity: count the girls in the study circle.*
5. **A school for girls** (1 November 1923): Diniyah Putri opens in a mosque; 71 students, many of them young mothers;
   Qur'an, Arabic and crafts. *Activity: build the school (tap the bricks). Activity: a choice question.*
6. **Not everyone agreed**: some people said girls did not need school; her brother died; she kept going.
   *Activity: hold to be patient.*
7. **The earthquake** (1926), told gently: the building fell; lessons went on in simple shelters; she travelled far to
   collect money, and a new school opened in 1928. *Activity: lead her journey (board). Activity: build it again.*
8. **On her own feet**: she would not take the colonial government's money; hundreds of girls; a college to train women
   teachers. *Activity: sort: what a good teacher does.*
9. **A new flag** (1945): when Indonesia became free, she raised the red-and-white flag in the school yard.
   *Activity: a choice question.* (The war, the Japanese, prison: parents' notes.)
10. **To Cairo**: the head of al-Azhar visited her school; after her Hajj she went to al-Azhar in Egypt, which gave her a
    title of honour never given to a woman before; her students could study there. *Activity: lead her journey to
    Makkah and Cairo (board).*
11. **Her school today**: a university for women (1967); she died in 1969; her school is still open, and Indonesia named
    her a National Hero. *Activity: tell her story in order.*

Parents' notes: her marriage and divorce; the Japanese occupation and her campaign against "comfort women"; the Dutch
prison; parliament, the PRRI and her arrest; the disputed dates; what "Syaikhah" means.
